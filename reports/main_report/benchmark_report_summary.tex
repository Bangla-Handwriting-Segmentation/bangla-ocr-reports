\title{\textbf{\large Benchmarking a Polygon-Output Swin--Mask R-CNN Segmentation Pipeline
Against Rectangle-Annotated Bengali HTR Baselines}\\[2pt]
\normalsize Executive Summary Report --- Three-Dataset Results\\[1pt]

\date{September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent We benchmark our Swin/Mask R-CNN pipeline (polygon output) against \bnd{}
\cite{jubaer2023bndrishti} (YOLOv5, rectangle output) on three datasets using a
literature-derived, format-agnostic protocol \cite{wolf2006detEval}. \swin{} wins
decisively on synthetic data, its own training domain (word F1 0.915 vs.\ 0.684);
\bnd{} wins on real BN-HTRd, its training domain; and \swin{} still wins on
BanglaWriting, ground neither system trained on (word F1 0.770 vs.\ 0.674). The
result is dataset-conditional, not a single winner. \bnd's edge on strict mAP@75 is
separately shown to be a rectangle-vs-polygon scoring artifact, not a real accuracy
gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and the Training-Domain Confound}

Our pipeline (Swin Transformer backbone, hierarchical Mask R-CNN, page$\to$line
$\to$word$\to$grapheme cascade) emits a 50-point directional polygon per instance,
closely tracing the true slant and curvature of handwritten Bengali text. \bnd{}
\cite{jubaer2023bndrishti} and the BN-HTRd dataset it extends \cite{rahman2022bnhtrd}
instead represent every line/word as an axis-aligned rectangle. A naive
rectangle-vs-rectangle IoU cannot score our polygon predictions without either erasing
their shape information (bbox reduction) or inventing an unprecedented ad hoc
comparison, so Section~2 adopts a literature-grounded, format-agnostic protocol.

\textbf{The training-domain confound.} \swin{} was trained \emph{exclusively} on the
synthetic \texttt{syn-hw} corpus; \bnd{} was trained \emph{exclusively} on the real
BN-HTRd corpus. Neither system has ever seen the other's training distribution. This
means a \texttt{syn-hw} benchmark is simultaneously an in-domain test for \swin{} and
an out-of-domain generalization test for \bnd{}, and the real-BN-HTRd benchmark is
exactly reversed -- neither alone is a neutral referee on ``which detector is
generally better.'' We therefore report \emph{three} datasets side by side: the two
home-turf corpora, plus BanglaWriting \cite{mridha2021banglawriting}, a third,
independent corpus neither system has ever trained on, added specifically to settle
the generalization question the first two cannot.

\section{Literature Review and Adopted Protocol}

We searched arXiv, IEEE Xplore, Springer Link, ACM DL, CVF Open Access, Semantic
Scholar, DBLP, and the Robust Reading Competition portal for methodology reconciling
polygon/quadrilateral/rectangle detection geometries under one evaluation protocol.
Twenty-six sources were retained. Two mechanisms recur: (1) Wolf \& Jolion's
object count/area \emph{split/merge} matching \cite{wolf2006detEval}, the basis of
``DetEval'' used in early ICDAR Robust Reading Competitions
\cite{shahab2011icdar2011,karatzas2013icdar2013,karatzas2015icdar2015}, which credits
a detection against several ground-truth regions once area-coverage constraints are
jointly satisfied ($\gtrsim$0.4 per-region, $\gtrsim$0.8 total), directly addressing
line/word granularity mismatches between cascaded models; and (2) rasterizing both
sides to a shared pixel-mask canvas and scoring pixel IoU, exactly as COCO's
\texttt{iou\_type="segm"} \cite{lin2014coco} and DIVA-HisDB \cite{simistira2017divahisdb}
do, sidestepping the polygon/rectangle format mismatch entirely. Total-Text
\cite{chng2017totaltext,chng2020totaltext} independently establishes polygon-native
ground truth for curved scene text, and Tightness-Aware IoU \cite{liu2019tiou} warns
that a single IoU threshold can conflate localization quality with shape-format
agreement -- a caution central to Section~3. Document layout analysis (PubLayNet
\cite{zhong2019publaynet}, DocLayNet \cite{pfitzmann2022doclaynet}, FUNSD
\cite{jaume2019funsd}) and the ICDAR Robust Reading lineage converge on P/R/F1 at
IoU$\geq$0.5 as the primary bar, with COCO-style mAP@50/75/mAP as secondary, stricter
numbers -- a convention we adopt. Critically, \emph{no} Bengali/Indic HTR paper
surveyed (BN-HTRd, \bnd{}, BanglaWriting, PHDIndic\_11 \cite{obaidullah2018phdindic11})
defines a protocol for comparing a rectangle-output system against a polygon-output
one; this report's methodology appears to be a first for the Bengali OCR space.

\textbf{Adopted protocol:} (i) implement Wolf \& Jolion split/merge matching
\cite{wolf2006detEval} underneath a strict greedy IoU$\geq$0.5 matcher and treat the
resulting P/R/F1 as this report's \textbf{headline metric}, so line/word granularity
mismatches do not silently inflate false positives/negatives; (ii) rasterize every
prediction/ground-truth region onto a shared downscaled canvas (768\,px longest side)
and score COCO-style mask mAP, so \swin's polygons are never penalized merely for not
being rectangles, reported alongside for completeness; (iii) additionally report
vector box-IoU mAP as a sanity check. For a pure rectangle-vs-rectangle case (\bnd{}
vs.\ ground truth), box-mode and mask-mode agree to within 1--2\%, confirming the
rasterization pipeline introduces no bias before it is trusted for the genuinely
mismatched polygon case.

\textbf{Why F1 leads and mAP follows.}\footnote{Full derivation and a consolidated mAP
reference table: Appendix A of the companion full technical report
(\texttt{report/benchmark\_report.tex}).} COCO mAP was built for generic object
detection, not small, densely-packed word boxes: IoU is a relative-area measure, so a
$\sim$30--60\,px word box swings sharply in IoU from sub-visual pixel misalignment, and
strict one-to-one matching scores every extra overlapping detection as a flat false
positive -- collapsing precision-driven AP whenever a system legitimately splits one
word into several correct sub-boxes, exactly the case split/merge matching handles
correctly instead (Table~1 above: switching \bnd{} from strict to split-merge matching
raises word F1 from 0.487 to 0.684 with \emph{no change to the underlying
predictions}). CLEval's character-level scoring \cite{baek2020cleval} and
Tightness-Aware IoU \cite{liu2019tiou} are further literature metrics purpose-built for
this exact setting; both are computed for BanglaWriting (Section~6) and pending for
\texttt{syn-hw}/BN-HTRd, with fuller detail in the companion full report.

\section{Part I: Synthetic Corpus (\texttt{syn-hw}) Results}

\texttt{syn-hw} is a synthetic handwritten Bengali page corpus (60{,}454 pages total,
6{,}045-page held-out validation split used here; 118{,}103 lines / 520{,}012 words in
that split), each page mixing several distinct synthetic handwriting fonts. Ground
truth is a bounding-box reduction of a native, pixel-precise 50-point ink-tracing
polygon -- computed specifically to match BN-HTRd/\bnd's own rectangle convention, not
because finer ground truth is unavailable. This is precisely why the rectangle ground
truth contains the ``empty margin'' quantified in Section~3.

Split-merge matching materially changes \bnd's own scores versus strict one-to-one
matching (word F1 0.487$\to$0.684, line F1 0.690$\to$0.776), confirming granularity
mismatches are common; mAP is unaffected since \texttt{MeanAveragePrecision} performs
its own internal one-to-many ranking. The full three-way comparison (both systems vs.\
ground truth, split-merge matching, mask-mode IoU, 6{,}045 pages, 2.53\,h on one
RTX~5090):

\section{Why Is BN-DRISHTI's mAP@75 Higher? (Investigation and Proof)}

A dedicated multi-stage investigation ruled out artifact explanations before accepting
a geometric one. \textbf{Resolution is not the cause}: mean matched-pair IoU is flat
across a $\sim$8$\times$ canvas-resolution sweep (384\,px to native $2480\times3510$)
for both systems, including at zero downscale. \textbf{Rasterization is not the
cause}: \texttt{cv2.fillPoly}'s rasterized pixel count agrees with the exact
shoelace-formula polygon area to within 1\% (mean ratio 0.991, $n=32$, zero malformed
polygons). \textbf{Box-mode reduction flips the ordering entirely in \swin's favor}
(reducing its polygon to its own bounding box turns the comparison into pure detection
confidence, where \swin{} is simply stronger) -- and a real handwritten line's tight
\swin{} polygon covers on average only 73.4\% of its own enclosing rectangle
(55.9--96.4\%, $n=32$).

To make the mechanism fully concrete and independent of either model's own geometry,
we Otsu-thresholded \cite{otsu1979threshold} the \emph{source page images} inside
every matched ground-truth box (60-page sample) to separate true ink pixels $I$ from
background/margin $M$. For matched prediction $P$: \emph{ink recall} $=|P\cap I|/|I|$
and \emph{background coverage} $=|P\cap M|/|M|$ -- a purely pixel-intensity measurement
using neither model's prediction as ground truth.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\footnotesize
\caption{Independent pixel-level ink/margin decomposition (60-page sample).}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrrr@{}}
\toprule
Task & System & Ink recall & Bg.\ coverage & $n$ \\
\midrule
Line & \bnd{}         & 0.958 & \textbf{0.857} & 1{,}180 \\
Line & \textbf{Swin} & 0.933 & 0.670 & 1{,}177 \\
Word & \bnd{}         & 0.988 & \textbf{0.979} & 6{,}363 \\
Word & \textbf{Swin} & 0.975 & 0.576 & 6{,}489 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Both systems recall real ink at a comparable, high rate (0.933--0.988): neither is
meaningfully ``missing'' text. The gap is entirely in background coverage --
\bnd's rectangle claims 85.7\%/97.9\% of each box's empty margin vs.\ \swin's
67.0\%/57.6\% -- the exact per-pixel mechanism behind the mAP@75 gap. A further
check: only 8.2\%/20.9\% (line/word) of a typical ground-truth box is actually ink, so
a hypothetically perfect ink-tight detector is structurally capped near IoU
$\approx$0.08 for lines -- strikingly close to \swin's observed line mAP@75 of 0.091.
\textbf{Verdict: mAP@75 under rectangle-only ground truth measures how
rectangle-shaped a prediction is, not how accurate it is}; mAP@50, split-merge P/R/F1,
and box-mode's own ordering are the metrics we recommend trusting, and \swin{} wins
clearly on all of them, for the right reasons.

\section{Part II: Real Corpus (BN-HTRd) Results}

\bnd's own paper reports headline numbers on real, photographed handwriting, so we
locate the actual extended BN-HTRd (v4.0) dataset already present locally
(\texttt{BN\_HTR/}, 3.0\,GB) and run the identical protocol on it. \textbf{Reading
\cite{jubaer2023bndrishti} directly}: its headline 99.97\%(line)/98\%(word) F-scores
are computed by \emph{manual visual comparison} (``Qualitative'' evaluation), not an
automated IoU protocol; its own automated (``Quantitative'') evaluation scores only
94.09\%(line)/16.0\%(word, hurt by a disclosed skew-correction coordinate bug), and
the paper candidly states this word evaluation ``does not do justice'' to the system.
Neither of \bnd's own two numbers is therefore directly comparable to our fully
automated protocol without this context.

The corpus contains 786 real pages/150 writers (matching the paper's reported 786
pages/14{,}383 lines); we use an existing writer-disjoint split whose test set (84
pages, 1{,}444 line boxes) is comparable in scale to the paper's own 75-image test set.
Word-level ground truth is only annotated per line crop, so we reconstruct 10{,}238
page-level word boxes by offsetting each line crop's word boxes by its line box's own
origin (verified exact via crop-dimension matching).

\bnd{} wins decisively on lines and edges ahead on words. \swin{} keeps a real
strength (word precision 0.828 vs.\ 0.524) but its recall collapses to 0.561 (vs.\
0.979 on synthetic data) -- it simply misses much real handwritten text. Our
automated reproduction gives \bnd{} a line F1 of 98.87\% (between the paper's own
94.09\% automated and 99.97\% manual figures) and word F1 of 68.52\% (far above the
paper's flawed 16.0\% automated figure but below its manual 98\%) -- evidence our
reproduction is methodologically sound. This is a textbook synthetic-to-real domain
gap: \swin{} was trained only on synthetic renders and never saw a real photographed
page, while \bnd{} was trained directly on this real corpus; it does not overturn Part
I, it contextualizes it.

\section{Part III: Neutral Corpus (BanglaWriting) Results}

Both results above are confounded by training data. To answer ``which detector
generalizes better'' directly, we benchmark on BanglaWriting
\cite{mridha2021banglawriting}: 260 single-page documents, 260 different writers, 52
scanner-digitized + 208 smartphone-photographed under real lighting, 21{,}234
manually-annotated word boxes -- \textbf{a corpus neither system has ever trained on}.
BanglaWriting carries no official line-level ground truth (only words), and rather
than fabricate line ground truth by heuristically clustering words -- our own derived
ground truth, not an independently verifiable one -- we score the word task only,
across all 260 pages.

\textbf{On data neither system has trained on, \swin{} still wins word-task F1}
(0.770 vs.\ 0.674), driven by much higher precision at somewhat lower recall than
\bnd{} -- a real, if narrower, win than its 59-point synthetic-data margin, not a tie
and not a reversal. mAP again collapses for \swin{} in mask mode (0.002 mAP@50) while
its box-mode mAP@50 stays respectable (0.353, close to \bnd's 0.500) -- confirmed via
a direct, pipeline-independent rasterized IoU check (172 matched instances): \swin's
mean polygon-vs-rectangle mask IoU is only 0.32--0.34 (0--1.2\% clearing 0.5) while its
box-mode IoU averages 0.51--0.57 (56--73\% clearing 0.5). This is the same mechanism
as Section~3, \emph{more pronounced} here because BanglaWriting's human-drawn boxes
carry more margin than \texttt{syn-hw}'s ink-derived ones -- independent confirmation
the mAP@75 effect is dataset-general, not specific to \texttt{syn-hw}'s ground-truth
construction.

\textbf{Literature cross-check (CLEval).}\footnote{Run via the official,
unmodified-algorithm upstream implementation \cite{baek2020cleval}; a small third-party
bug (a missing attribute in CLEval's polygon box type) had to be patched to run correctly
on our polygon predictions -- an upstream implementation bug, not an adjustment to our
data. TIoU \cite{liu2019tiou} was also run on BanglaWriting but is not reported as a
system comparison here: it gates its continuous tightness score behind a hard
IoU$\geq$0.5 base match, and \swin's raw ink-tracing polygon mostly fails that gate
against BanglaWriting's box-only ground truth for the same geometric reason mask-mode
mAP@50 collapsed above, making a polygon-vs-box TIoU comparison structurally unfair here
rather than informative; a fair TIoU comparison is possible on \texttt{syn-hw}, which has
real polygon ground truth, once that run completes.}

CLEval's character-level F1 (0.639 \bnd{}, 0.799 Swin) tracks the split-merge headline

from an officially-maintained protocol with no dependence on this project's own matching

CLEval runs, and \texttt{syn-hw}'s polygon-vs-polygon TIoU run, are pending.

\textbf{Resolving the confound}: \swin{} wins decisively on its own synthetic training
domain, \bnd{} and \swin{} are near parity on BN-HTRd (\bnd's home turf), and on the
one corpus foreign to \emph{both}, \swin{} wins again by a smaller but real margin.
The most defensible summary: \swin{} is the stronger raw word detector by F1 in two of
three conditions, including the unconfounded one, but this advantage narrows away
from its own training distribution and never extends to mask-mode mAP on real,
non-synthetic ground truth, where the rectangle-vs-polygon geometric penalty
dominates regardless of dataset.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{(0)} Neither home-turf dataset alone is a neutral referee -- Part III exists
precisely to avoid averaging two biased numbers. \textbf{(1)} Matching strategy
materially changes \bnd's own reported scores (word F1 understated by $\sim$20 points
under strict matching); its own paper's headline 99.97\%/98\% figures are themselves
manually adjudicated, not automated, complicating any comparison to automated
protocols including ours. \textbf{(2)} The mAP@75 result is a caution against a single
headline metric across systems with different native output geometries, as
Tightness-Aware IoU \cite{liu2019tiou} argues generally. \textbf{(3)} The real-BN-HTRd
ranking reversal is a genuine, deployment-relevant synthetic-to-real domain gap for
\swin{}, not yet closed by fine-tuning. \textbf{(4)} BanglaWriting shows this gap is
tied to BN-HTRd's specific capture conditions (or the line task specifically), not
broad evidence \swin{} is worse at handwriting detection once training-domain
advantage is removed -- on a third, differently-captured real corpus it is not.
\textbf{(5)} The same neutral corpus reproduces the mAP@75 artifact independently and
\emph{more severely}, confirming it as a general property of comparing polygon output
to rectangle ground truth.

\section{Conclusion}

On synthetic \texttt{syn-hw} data (our training domain), \swin{} beats \bnd{}
decisively on every fair metric. On real BN-HTRd (\bnd's training domain), the
ranking reverses on lines and narrows to a near-tie on words. On BanglaWriting,
ground neither system trained on, \swin{} still wins word-task F1 (0.770 vs.\ 0.674).
\textbf{The honest summary is dataset-conditional, not a single winner}: each system
looks strongest on its own training domain, and \swin{} remains the stronger raw word
detector on the one dataset built to be unbiased toward either. \bnd's edge on
mask-mode mAP@75 is a rectangle-vs-polygon geometric artifact, independently
confirmed via pixel-level analysis, not a real detection-quality gap.
